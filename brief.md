# Scopwise Brand and Website Brief

## 1. Project Definition

Scopwise is a Poland/EU-based enterprise SaaS platform for organizations deploying internal copilots, workflow agents, and automation bots. It gives security, platform, compliance, and AI teams a defensible answer to a deceptively simple question:

> What can this agent access, what can it do, and how can we prove it?

Scopwise is not an agent builder, chatbot platform, generic AI-governance suite, or model-observability tool. It is the access-governance and evidence layer between enterprise agents and the data, applications, tools, and actions they can reach.

## 2. Positioning

### Category

Agent access governance for enterprise AI.

### Positioning statement

Scopwise helps enterprises discover, control, and prove what every AI agent can access and do, so internal copilots and automation agents pass security review and reach production safely.

### Primary message

**Prove what your agents can access.**

### Supporting message

Map every connection. Set enforceable boundaries. Produce review-ready evidence.

### Product architecture

- **Scopwise Map:** reveal every agent-to-data, agent-to-app, and agent-to-action relationship.
- **Scopwise Rules:** define and communicate allowed, conditional, and blocked behavior.
- **Scopwise Review:** produce a clear, reviewable record for security, legal, compliance, and business owners.

### Messaging pillars

- **Visibility:** see the complete access path, including inherited and indirect access.
- **Control:** establish least-privilege boundaries for data, applications, and actions.
- **Evidence:** replace assumptions and screenshots with a reviewable system of record.
- **Speed:** shorten security review without weakening it.
- **Confidence:** expand agent adoption while keeping ownership and consequences clear.

## 3. Audience

### Primary buyers

- Chief Information Security Officers and security engineering leaders
- Heads of AI, enterprise automation, and platform engineering
- Identity and access management leaders
- Risk, compliance, privacy, and internal audit teams

### Daily users

- Security reviewers
- AI platform engineers
- Automation and workflow teams
- Application owners
- Compliance analysts

### Their current questions

- Which systems can this agent read from or write to?
- Can it approve, delete, purchase, publish, or trigger downstream workflows?
- Did access expand after a connector or policy changed?
- Who approved this boundary and on what evidence?
- What is the blast radius if the agent behaves incorrectly?
- Can we reproduce the evidence used during review?

## 4. Product Outcome

The website should make a complex governance problem understandable within the first viewport and credible within the first three sections. Visitors should leave with three ideas:

1. Agent access is a connected system, not a list of permissions.
2. Scopwise makes that system visible and controllable.
3. Scopwise produces evidence enterprises can review and trust.

The primary conversion is **Request a demo**. Secondary conversions are **Explore the platform**, **View a sample review**, and product-page navigation.

## 5. Brand Character

### Personality

- Precise, calm, observant, and quietly confident
- European, responsible, and modern without using compliance cliches
- Creative enough to make infrastructure memorable
- Human in language, rigorous in evidence
- Premium without looking luxurious for its own sake

### Creative idea

**The evidence atlas.**

Scopwise turns invisible agent access into a legible world of routes, boundaries, decisions, and proof. The brand language should use paths, intersections, layers, annotations, coordinates, review marks, and soft fields of color. It should feel closer to a beautifully designed technical map than a cybersecurity command center.

### Avoid

- Generic shields, locks, robots, brains, sparkles, and glowing AI spheres
- Neon-on-black cyberpunk styling as the main identity
- Heavy condensed display fonts or oversized block typography
- Blue-only enterprise palettes
- Excessive gradients, glassmorphism, floating cards, and decorative dashboards
- Claims such as "total security," "zero risk," or unsupported compliance certifications

## 6. Visual Direction

### Selected direction

The approved Scopwise system combines the three explored territories into one intentional hierarchy:

- **Primary brand and homepage hero: Prism Ledger.** Use the lavender-led access-boundary ledger, restrained left-aligned typography, pale blue-white canvas, and horizontal semantic bands as the dominant first impression.
- **Map and discovery storytelling: Evidence Garden.** Use detailed agent-to-data-to-app-to-action paths, pastel node families, owner columns, and assumed-versus-actual access comparisons.
- **Review, trust, and conversion: Signal Atelier.** Use warm paper, apricot exception states, mint approvals, human ownership, evidence tables, and editorial conversion moments.

These are not separate themes. They share the same ink color, typography, spacing system, grid, radii, icon language, and navigation. Only the supporting surface color and information density change with the story.

### Homepage visual hierarchy

1. **Hero:** Prism Ledger access boundary with the headline “Boundaries people can see.”
2. **Problem and Map:** Evidence Garden visibility map with the message “You can’t govern what you can’t see.”
3. **Rules:** Lavender and butter policy bands showing read, write, approve, trigger, delete, conditional, and blocked states.
4. **Review:** Signal Atelier evidence journal with owners, changed permissions, decisions, exceptions, and timestamps.
5. **Closing CTA:** Warm paper and apricot emphasis with “Bring your next agent to review with evidence.”

### Overall art direction

Use a light, editorial SaaS system with pastel color fields and dark ink typography. Color should help explain access states and product relationships, not merely decorate the page. Alternate generous warm-white sections with pale blue, lavender, mint, apricot, and butter fields. One dark-ink section may be used for contrast near the evidence or review chapter.

Visual density should increase as the story progresses: the hero begins calm, the problem section reveals complexity, and the product chapters resolve that complexity into an ordered system.

### Typography

Exactly two Google Font families:

- `--font-heading: "Instrument Serif"` for expressive editorial phrases, italic emphasis, pull quotes, and selective product storytelling.
- `--font-body: "Manrope"` for navigation, product UI, body copy, labels, data, and the structural portion of headlines.

Typography should feel articulate rather than loud. Use regular and medium weights as the default. Semibold is reserved for compact labels, buttons, and high-value interface states. Avoid extra-bold weights.

#### Desktop type scale

| Token | Size / line height | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Display | 68 / 72 px | Hero only; regular weight; maximum two to three lines |
| H1 | 56 / 60 px | Product-page hero |
| H2 | 44 / 52 px | Major storyline chapters |
| H3 | 32 / 40 px | Product and use-case sections |
| H4 | 24 / 32 px | Compact feature headings |
| Lead | 20 / 32 px | Hero and section introductions |
| Body large | 18 / 28 px | Primary explanatory copy |
| Body | 16 / 24 px | Standard copy and UI |

Letter spacing remains `0`. Headline creativity comes from typeface contrast, italics, line breaks, and color rather than compressed tracking or extreme scale.

### Color balance

- 45% warm white
- 20% pale sky

- 5% apricot
- 5% butter
- 5% dark ink contrast

## 7. Layout System

### Desktop frame

- Primary design viewport: `1440 px`
- Content container: `1312 px`
- Outer margin at 1440 px: `64 px`
- Grid: 12 columns
- Grid gutter: `24 px`
- Long-form text width: 6 columns or approximately `620 px`
- Product visual width: 6 to 8 columns depending on chapter
- Navbar height: `80 px`

### Spacing scale

Use only `4, 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96 px`.

- `4 px`: icon optical corrections and tightly related metadata
- `8 px`: label-to-value and compact control spacing
- `16 px`: control padding and compact content groups
- `24 px`: default grid gap, card padding, heading-to-copy spacing
- `32 px`: related content blocks and form groups
- `48 px`: subsection separation
- `64 px`: page gutters, navigation groups, large component spacing
- `96 px`: section rhythm and major chapter spacing

### Section rhythm

- Standard section padding: `96 px` top and bottom
- Narrative/sticky chapters: minimum `192 px` visible content padding, expressed as two `96 px` intervals
- Section heading to content: `48 px`
- Heading to supporting paragraph: `24 px`
- Paragraph to primary action: `32 px`
- Repeated product items: `24 px` gaps

### Alignment principles

- Every section aligns to the same 12-column grid.
- Headlines, diagrams, and product surfaces share clear vertical anchors.
- Product visuals may extend to the viewport edge only when the overflow supports the scroll narrative.
- Avoid centered copy except for brief transitions and closing conversion moments.
- Do not place page sections inside floating cards.
- Cards are reserved for review objects, evidence records, testimonials, pricing plans, and repeated entities.
- Card radius is `8 px` maximum; buttons use `6-8 px` radius.

## 8. Logo Direction

The wordmark should be calm and highly legible: `Scopwise` in Manrope Medium with a custom treatment around the `o`, `w`, or the `wise` transition. The symbol should communicate scope plus evidence without becoming a literal target or shield.

Preferred conceptual routes:

- Two offset boundaries intersecting to create a visible access window.
- A route entering a framed scope and leaving as an approved evidence mark.
- Three aligned layers representing Map, Rules, and Review.

Required variants:

- Primary horizontal wordmark
- Compact symbol
- Dark-background reverse lockup
- Monochrome black and white versions
- Favicon at 32 px and 16 px

The final logo should be vector-authored after one brand direction is selected. Image generation may be used for exploration, not as the final vector master.

## 9. Homepage Storyline

### 1. Transparent navigation

Wordmark, Product dropdown, Solutions dropdown, Pricing, Company, Contact, Sign In, and Request Demo. Navigation remains transparent in the hero and gains a subtle warm-white surface after scrolling.

### 2. Hero: the access path

Headline: **Prove what your agents can access.**

Supporting copy: Map every connection, set clear boundaries, and bring review-ready evidence to security, legal, and business owners.

Visual: a calm agent-access route connecting an agent to data, applications, and actions. As the visitor scrolls, the route reveals hidden inherited permissions and changed states.

### 3. The review gap

Headline: **The agent works. The evidence does not.**

A pinned section explains why deployments stall: unclear data reach, uncertain write actions, permission drift, missing ownership, and no reproducible review record.

### 4. Product sequence

A horizontal or sticky three-chapter sequence:

- Map the real access surface.
- Set boundaries people can understand.
- Review changes with evidence.

### 5. Scopwise Map

Show an access graph moving from a tangled state to a legible relationship map. Include data, applications, actions, owners, environments, and inherited access.

### 6. Scopwise Rules

Show a boundary layer applied to the map. States include read, write, approve, trigger, delete, conditional, and blocked.

### 7. Scopwise Review

Show a review record containing scope, changes, exceptions, owners, decisions, and evidence timestamps.

### 8. Use cases

Internal copilots, workflow agents, automation bots, regulated operations, and enterprise AI platform teams.

### 9. Trust and EU readiness

Explain least privilege, auditability, GDPR-aware operations, evidence retention, and deployment choices. Only display certifications and customer logos that are verified.

### 10. Proof

Customer logo marquee using real approved companies, outcome-led testimonials, and one concise case study.

### 11. Pricing

Enterprise-oriented plans with clear differences in agent count, integrations, review workflows, retention, deployment, and support. Avoid invented prices until commercial packaging is approved.

### 12. Closing conversion

Headline: **Bring your next agent to review with evidence.**

Primary action: Request a demo. Secondary action: View a sample review.

## 10. Scroll and Motion System

- Lenis provides smooth scrolling while preserving native input behavior.
- GSAP ScrollTrigger controls sticky chapters, path progression, horizontal product sequencing, and restrained text reveals.
- Framer Motion handles dropdowns, buttons, form states, and small interface transitions.
- Motion illustrates cause and effect: a connection appears, a rule changes it, and a review records the decision.
- Default reveal duration: `500-700 ms`.
- Default easing: smooth deceleration, not elastic motion.
- Parallax offset remains subtle, generally under `48 px`.
- Text reveals occur by line or phrase; avoid animating every word.
- Reduced-motion mode removes scrubbed transformations and presents all information in normal document flow.
- Three.js is optional and should only be used if a spatial access map materially improves understanding.

## 13. Content Voice

### Do

- Use direct verbs: map, define, review, prove, trace, compare, approve.
- Explain consequences in plain language.
- Treat security and compliance readers as knowledgeable peers.
- Use short editorial phrases to create rhythm between technical sections.

### Do not

- Use breathless AI language or vague future-facing claims.
- Call everything intelligent, autonomous, revolutionary, or seamless.
- Overload the page with feature lists.
- Imply certification, customer adoption, or regulatory approval without evidence.

## 14. Trust Requirements

- Use only approved customer and partner logos.
- Clearly separate product capability from legal or compliance claims.
- Provide a security page and future Trust Center entry point.
- Include a credible Poland/EU company address after legal details are confirmed.
- Use realistic forms, success states, and contact expectations.
- Include privacy, cookie, and data-processing information before production launch.

## 16. Acceptance Criteria

- Scopwise is clearly understood as agent access governance within the first viewport.
- The visual identity is recognizable without relying on generic security imagery.
- Typography is expressive but never bulky or difficult to scan.
- Pastel colors retain sufficient text contrast and have semantic meaning.
- All pages use the same navigation, footer, spacing, typography, and interaction system.
- No third-party template branding or placeholder content remains.
- Scroll effects improve comprehension and do not block normal navigation.
- Product diagrams remain legible at every target viewport.
- Forms, menus, links, buttons, and primary journeys work.
- Reduced motion, keyboard focus, and accessible color states are supported.

## 17. Build Recommendation

Use Next.js with TypeScript, GSAP ScrollTrigger, Lenis, Framer Motion, Lucide icons, and Vercel. Build the static responsive structure and product diagrams before introducing scroll choreography. Motion is the final explanatory layer, not the foundation of the layout.
